National Palace Museum｜Documentary on a cultural guide course for new immigrants

Commissioned by the National Palace Museum｜Filming began in 2019, released in 2023

The National Palace Museum runs a course that trains new immigrants to guide visitors from their home countries through the collection, in their own languages. My job was to make a documentary about the course.

I couldn't find a main character for the film. I felt it needed one to be worth watching. Otherwise it would be just another event record.

Then the organisers introduced a Vietnamese student who lived in Changhua. To get to class she had to leave home at four or five in the morning, and sometimes she came up to Taipei the night before.

After a little small talk, I asked a question that didn't really matter for the film:

“It's so far. Isn't it boring to come all this way alone? Why not bring a friend and make it a trip to Taipei?”

She suddenly cried. She said the other women from her hometown laughed at her: with that much time, why not go and earn money? But she simply loved learning.

That was how I found my main character.

ResultWorldFest-Houston International Film Festival: silver award for documentary, gold award for adult education

National Theater & Concert Hall, outreach programme 《藝術出走》｜For them, art is a service

Documentary for an outreach programme

The National Theater & Concert Hall runs an outreach programme called 《藝術出走》, which takes the arts to rural towns: several large productions a year, plus touring workshops. It depends on a great deal of sponsorship.

This was the first time I had taken on a client of this size. I was nervous, and I took it very seriously.

We filmed in several places over several weeks. For the first cut I used the best technique I had at the time and picked the shots with the fullest emotion, the most beautiful images and the liveliest faces. I made it as polished and good-looking as I could.

I sent it in. The reply: not enough tension.

It was an abstract comment, and I thought about it for a long time. Then it occurred to me: if this is a story, there must be difficulty, and there must be overcoming it.

So I opened the film with doubt, contradiction and difficulty, the things I used to assume a client wouldn't want. Only after that did I show how the team got through them, and the ideas they worked from.

ResultThis time the reply was that the sponsor had been moved by it, and had decided to give more, and for longer.

Sangpuy in concert｜Root, Trunk, Leaf: a trilogy

National Theater & Concert Hall｜Three short films before the concert

Sangpuy is a Golden Melody Award-winning singer. He was about to give his first concert at the National Theater & Concert Hall, and I was asked to make the promotional films.

But the only shoot arranged at that point was one of his singing-and-sharing sessions.

My judgement was that one sharing session alone could not make a promotional film.

I asked around and found there would be a press conference beforehand, where he would sing a passage unaccompanied. I thought that mattered, so I recorded it.

Then I kept asking myself: why does he sing at all? What is he trying to convey? I pulled together the interview from the sharing session and a lot of background material and press coverage, and made one film.

The client watched it and suggested expanding one film into three, named after a tree. Root: why he sings. Trunk: his performance, so people hear his voice. Leaf: where he wants to go. He wants to use song to bring Indigenous culture to a wider audience.

ResultSeveral of the concerts sold out. Whether these three films made any difference, I don't know.

National Theater & Concert Hall, “Not Afraid of Sun and Rain”｜Three short films and a director's cut

National Theater & Concert Hall｜Promotional shorts before the performance

A new work by Bulareyaung Dance Company at the National Theater & Concert Hall. It needed promotional shorts before opening.

There was a performance date, a title and a creative concept. But the company's artistic director still wasn't sure what the piece itself would be.

He took the whole company to Taitung to train together, to experience Amis culture and to look for inspiration.

Our difficulty was this: nobody knew what the performance would turn out to be, and the promotional films still had to be made first.

So we focused on the dancers challenging themselves. We used intense, symbolic, poetic images of bodies colliding with nature to raise the audience's expectations for the piece. To our surprise, it turned into three promotional films.

Later, on my own, I cut them into a single director's cut.

ResultThe performance was postponed because of COVID-19. But the dancers still found their own answer.

Dimple Tseng, CPA｜The accountant who grows alongside her clients

A film shown before her talk

Dimple Tseng runs her own accounting practice. Her clients had all come by word of mouth. At the time she wanted a change and planned to give a talk at a Rotary Club. I offered to make a film to play before the talk, so the audience would get to know her first.

At first I didn't know which direction to take either, so I began by interviewing her. Two things stood out.

First, her office is inside her client's company, on the upper level of a mezzanine. Every day she walks in and climbs a tall staircase. Second, she loves hiking and running marathons.

As we talked further I learned that the most representative thing in her career so far was accompanying this client from nothing. Beyond the accounting, she had given them a great deal of financial advice.

I suggested the angle “the accountant who grows with her client from small to large”, filmed through mountain climbing and that staircase. She agreed.

After the film was finished, she didn't adopt that angle straight away.

After watching the film, she rediscovered the weight of what she had done. How to offer that kind of service again, systematically, was something she felt she had to think through carefully before she could design a process able to carry that responsibility.
